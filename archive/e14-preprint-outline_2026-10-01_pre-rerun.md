**Novelty note (rewritten against specific prior work; corrected per peer-review MA10):**

- *Modality dropout for missing-sensor robustness is not novel.* ModDrop (Neverova et al., TPAMI 2015) established the technique; CognifySSL v2.0 (*Sensors* 2026) applies it to WESAD IMU/ECG/EDA/TEMP; PULSE (arXiv 2510.24058) includes WESAD modality-dropout ablations and reports 0.994 AUROC without EDA at inference via privileged-information distillation. Prior wording claiming "has received little attention" was an overclaim.
- *Learnable modality gating for missing-modality robustness is also not novel.* SimMLM (arXiv 2507.19264) uses "a dynamic, learnable gating mechanism that automatically adjusts each modality's contribution in both full and partial modality settings" and reports ECE across 15 missing/full modality settings. That is prior art for *gating* in the missing-modality setting; the gated architecture in Section 4.2 is not novel in kind. (Earlier drafts attributed the gating idea to this paper by omission; corrected per MA10.)
- *Calibration under missing modalities is also not novel as a framing.* Ma et al., "Calibrating Multimodal Learning" (ICML 2023, arXiv 2306.01265) treats calibration under modality corruption/removal as the central problem and shows that model confidence can *increase* when modalities are corrupted, proposing the Calibrating Multimodal Learning (CML) regularizer under the principle that confidence should not increase when a modality is removed. SimMLM reports ECE under missing modalities (above). **Lechuga López, Shamout & Rudner (arXiv 2603.02719) studies calibration and selective prediction in multimodal clinical ICU condition classification, including class-dependent miscalibration; it is NOT a missing-modality paper. Prior drafts described it that way; the correction, per MA10, is to cite it for the *calibration + selective-prediction* framing and to point to its finding that aggregate calibration does not guarantee useful selective prediction.** Ma et al.'s CML regularizer is a direct baseline that should be cited and included.
- *What is defensibly new, narrower than previously claimed:*
  1. **Empirical calibration-under-missingness evidence for wearable physiological proxies (ECG, EDA) specifically, against stronger baselines (M9) than prior work**, not a new framing of calibration under missingness. This value is conditional on the pre-stated falsification rule in 5.2 and on the baseline set actually being run; if the rule fires, the paper's contribution becomes a candid negative result against stronger baselines, which is itself an underrepresented class of result in this literature.
  2. **A protocol for measuring calibration across sensor-availability tiers**, demonstrated on either real WESAD chest-vs-wrist tiers (MA4 fix) or, if raw-signal extraction is not completed, on simulated tiers labelled as a methodological proposal only. Fairness in ubiquitous health sensing already names device type as a heterogeneity axis (arXiv 2603.13373, citing Adler et al. 2024, Yfantidou et al. 2023; Zinzuwadia & Singh, Lancet Digit Health 2022). The narrower open gap — *calibration* equity across sensor-availability tiers — is modest but legitimate; the strong word "equity" is dropped until a real-hardware demonstration exists.
  3. **A staged dataset-identity audit protocol integrating existing standard components** (classifier two-sample test / Lopez-Paz & Oquab 2017; identity-probe permutation test / Chaibub Neto et al. arXiv 1712.03120, originally for *subject* identity and here adapted to *dataset* identity; MMD with subject-block permutation; CORAL alignment; shortcut-learning line from Zech et al. 2018, Rabanser et al. 2019, DeGrave et al. 2021, npj Digital Medicine 2024) *in the proxy-vs-proxy wearable setting* (WESAD × MIT-BIH). The components are standard; the integration is the contribution. This value is conditional on fixing M1 (Step 3 model mislabel — done in `audit/step3_shortcut.py`; PATHOLOGY_LIKELY verdict retired), M6/C2 (pre-registration/`--fast` deviations), MA7 (Step 4 CI-rule mismatch — fixed in `audit/step4_identity_probe.py::_verdict_step4`), and MA8 (full-permutation rerun with Holm correction).

## 1. Introduction
- Clinical motivation: symptom overlap between panic and cardiac events; real-time onset differentiation is the eventual goal
- **State immediately**: no public dataset pairs confirmed panic attacks and confirmed cardiac events on the same subjects with the same sensor modalities. This paper does not attempt that comparison.
- Reframe contribution: given that ceiling, what *can* be rigorously established using proxy data? → calibration behaviour and representation-learning behaviour under realistic deployment constraints (missing sensors, device heterogeneity, small-N).
- Three-angle framing (extends the two-angle deck): Angle 1 — hunt for one explainable mechanism (H2a). Angle 2 — build a system robust regardless of whether Angle 1 succeeds (H-B1/H-C1). **Angle 3 — audit the cross-dataset proxy pairing *before* any cross-dataset claim is made, so the H-B2 null is not inherited by default (per peer-review Section 5 contradiction #4; the earlier Angle 3 wording "the audit that explains why [H-B2 failed]" was a post-hoc causal story retracted in 5.4).** State plainly that the paper reports all three, including the H-B2 null and the audit, without claiming the audit explains the null.
- **Generalizable claim sentence (interval-supported, rewritten per peer-review C1 and Section 5 contradictions #1, #5, #6, #7, #14):** "Calibration under missing modalities has been framed and addressed in general multimodal learning (Ma et al., ICML 2023; SimMLM, 2025) and in multimodal clinical ICU classification with selective prediction (Lechuga López et al., 2026); empirical evidence for wearable physiological signals against strong reduced-sensor baselines remains limited. On 15 WESAD subjects under 5-fold subject-level cross-validation (CV) we report point estimates in which the gated model's ECE_reduced/ECE_complete ratio is smaller than a LightGBM baseline's (4.23× → 2.21× → 1.61× for LightGBM / gated no-dropout / gated + dropout p=0.5). The three per-condition 95% CIs each include 1 (no degradation) and overlap one another; the lowest LightGBM bound is 0.18 (so missing EDA improved calibration on at least one fold), i.e. the CI *includes* 1 rather than lying below it (correcting an earlier wording that said 'below 1'). Dropout vs no-dropout within the gated architecture is directionally consistent across all five folds (Wilcoxon W=0 at the n=5 exact two-sided floor p=0.0625); the uncorrected paired t gives p=0.056, and the Nadeau–Bengio-corrected paired t gives p≈0.15 (so the inferential status is 'direction consistent across folds, no statistical separation at n=15'; the earlier Limitations wording p=0.056 without the correction is superseded). Signal within WESAD is concentrated in heart rate (`hrv_mean_nn` carries ~50% of LightGBM SHAP mass; removing it drops within-WESAD C3 LOSO AUC from 0.899 to 0.674) and EDA (~44%); HRV beyond heart rate contributes ~5%. The main empirical question for the venue version is therefore not whether the gated model has a smaller point ratio than a standard-model baseline — it does — but whether it beats a per-configuration ECG-only LightGBM and a heart-rate-only model on *absolute* reduced-condition Brier, debiased ECE and AUROC (see pre-stated falsification rule in 5.2). Under *simulated* device tiers, the gated model's accuracy loss (7.5%) is smaller than the baseline's (10.6%); we characterise this as 'a smaller accuracy loss under simulated tiers,' not as equity, calibration-robust behaviour or favouring lower-tier users."

## 2. Related Work / Structural Gap
- Existing wearable panic-prediction work (e.g., next-day risk prediction, single-channel HR) — contrast with this paper's onset-level, multimodal approach
- **Modality-dropout / missing-sensor robustness literature — cite specifically, not generically.** ModDrop (Neverova et al., TPAMI 2015, arXiv 1501.00102) is the canonical modality-dropout technique. CognifySSL v2.0 (*Sensors* 2026) applies stochastic modality dropout to WESAD IMU/ECG/EDA/TEMP under missing-input evaluation. PULSE (arXiv 2510.24058) includes modality-dropout ablations on WESAD and achieves 0.994 AUROC without EDA at inference via privileged-information distillation — this is a direct baseline this paper must compare against. State what each prior paper measures (accuracy / AUROC / F1 under missingness) and the delta this paper claims.
- **Calibration-under-missingness literature — cite directly.** Ma et al., "Calibrating Multimodal Learning" (ICML 2023, arXiv 2306.01265) is the central prior work: it frames calibration under modality corruption as the problem and shows confidence can increase when modalities are corrupted, proposing a Calibrating Multimodal Learning (CML) regularizer. SimMLM (arXiv 2507.19264) reports ECE under missing modalities. Lechuga López et al. (arXiv 2603.02719) analyses calibration and selective prediction in multimodal clinical classifiers. The CML regularizer should be considered as a baseline alongside this paper's gated architecture.
  **Positioning sentence (corrected):** "Modality dropout for missing-sensor robustness in wearable settings is established (ModDrop; CognifySSL v2.0; PULSE), and calibration under modality corruption is directly framed by Ma et al. (ICML 2023) with ECE reported by SimMLM. This paper contributes empirical wearable-physiological evidence on calibration under missing modalities against stronger baselines (NaN-aware LightGBM, EDA-NaN-masked training, per-tier models, and ideally the Ma et al. CML regularizer and PULSE-style privileged-information distillation — see M9 to-do list), reported with CV-dependence-corrected inference on 15 WESAD subjects."
- **Device-tier equity literature — cite the heterogeneity-axis prior work.** Prior ubiquitous-health-sensing fairness work (arXiv 2603.13373, citing Adler et al. 2024 and Yfantidou et al. 2023) names device type as a heterogeneity axis where parity methods can miss subgroup failures; Zinzuwadia & Singh, *Lancet Digital Health* 2022;4(12):e856-e857, document that wearables are not used as widely in low-income and minority populations. The narrower open gap — *calibration* equity across sensor-availability tiers — is this paper's methodological framing, but it is a measurement proposal under simulated tiers, not a demonstrated clinical-fairness finding.
- **Shortcut learning / dataset identity in clinical models** — Zech et al. (PLOS Medicine, 2018) showed pneumonia CNNs robustly identifying the source hospital system, warning that internal test performance overstates real-world performance. Rabanser et al. (NeurIPS 2019, "Failing Loudly") established two-sample tests on classifier-reduced representations as the standard shift-detection tool. DeGrave et al. (Nature MI, 2021) documented shortcut learning in COVID-19 chest X-ray models. Our dataset-identity audit (Section 5.6) applies this line of work to wearable proxy-data comparisons — WESAD × MIT-BIH — where dataset and clinical class are structurally collinear.
- **H-B2's structural finding**: systematic search confirms no dataset exists pairing EDA with confirmed cardiac events — a citable contribution about the field's data infrastructure, not just a limitation of this study. This is the *reason* the audit is necessary: the only cross-dataset comparison available is dataset-collinear by construction.
- Position against prior wearable-panic literature's reliance on self-report ground truth (methods draft Section 6) as a design principle this work is built toward, even if not yet achievable on proxy data

## 4. Methods
### 4.1 Preprocessing & Feature Extraction
- ECG (HRV time/frequency-domain, morphology), EDA (tonic/phasic decomposition). Respiration (EDR) is in the pre-registered feature plan but not emitted by the current pipeline (see 5.2b modality-substitution note and Section 7 Limitations — WESAD does contain respiration via RespiBAN, so this is an extraction-pipeline gap, not a dataset limitation).
- **Window length and overlap (document explicitly, per reviewer).** State the window length used for all HRV feature extraction and whether windows overlap. The audit's 60-s MIT-BIH windows and the ~445 WESAD windows across 15 subjects over Schmidt et al. 2018's ~30-min protocol are consistent with ~60-s windows. **At a 60-s window length, HRV frequency-domain features are not interpretable.** The Task Force 1996 guidance (Circulation 93:1043) says "recording of approximately 1 minute is needed to assess the HF components of HRV, while approximately 2 minutes are needed to address the LF component," and prefers 5-minute recordings. Therefore:
  - LF power is unreliable at this window length; its SHAP of 0.000 (Section 5.5) is consistent with that.
  - The IG claim that LF moves from rank 8 to rank 5 under eda_missing, previously described as "a plausible autonomic-regulation mechanism", should be **removed or tagged as unreliable** given the window-length constraint.
  - If windows overlap, that further inflates effective n and must be stated.
- **Feature-extraction specifics to document** (TRIPOD+AI relevant, per reviewer Section 7): the R-peak detector used, the EDA decomposition method (cvxEDA? NeuroKit?), filtering settings, mains notch, and whether SQI/artifact gating is implemented or deferred.
- **Integrated Gradients baseline and completeness (document explicitly, per reviewer minor concerns).** State the IG baseline (zero vector, feature mean, blurred, or per-modality zero), the number of integration steps, and verify the IG completeness axiom (sum of attributions ≈ model(x) − model(baseline)). IG rank shifts are sensitive to the baseline choice; a zero baseline combined with a closed modality gate makes that modality's IG = 0 by construction (as reported in Section 5.5 under eda_missing and ecg_zeroed), which is uninformative.
- **SHAP on highly correlated HRV features** (SDNN, RMSSD, pNN50, HF) spreads attribution unpredictably across the correlated group. Report **grouped (modality-level or HRV-family-level) attributions** alongside per-feature SHAP, or use a correlation-aware variant.
- SQI/artifact gating — note current status (built vs. not yet built) honestly if included.

- **Repeated subject-level CV for the venue version (per peer-review MA5):** 20× repeated subject-level 5-fold CV with the Bouckaert–Frank (PAKDD 2004) corrected t-statistic, plus a subject-level sign-flip permutation test on per-subject Brier and log-loss differences (n=15, exact sign-flip over 2^15 = 32,768 sign patterns, giving a floor near 6×10⁻⁵ instead of the single-5-fold Wilcoxon floor of 0.0625). Implemented in `run_repeated_cv.py` (new, see pending-experiments section).
- **Calibration-fit protocol (per peer-review MA6):** Platt scaling is fit on the training fold only (never on test subjects), using the pooled complete-modality training data; a single Platt model is applied at both the complete and reduced test tiers so that any inter-condition difference reflects the model, not the recalibrator. Isotonic is reported alongside as a sensitivity check, nested within the training fold. Both pre- and post-recalibration ECE/Brier are reported. This closes the leakage ambiguity flagged by MA6; the full protocol specification is in `run_metrics.py` (new, see pending-experiments section).

### 5.1 Mechanism test (H2a) — null result
- EDA-HR coupling lag: window-level n = 445 across 15 subjects, Cohen's d = 0.06, p = 0.94.
- **"Well-powered" is false at the subject level (per reviewer M5).** The unit of generalization is the subject (n = 15), not the window. Windows within a subject are strongly correlated, so n = 445 is pseudoreplicated. At n = 15 subjects, a paired-subject design has 80% power at α = 0.05 only for effects d_z ≥ ~0.78 — so a non-significant window-level d = 0.06 cannot be read as "evidence of absence". To claim a null, replace the window-level test with a **subject-level analysis**: aggregate the coupling-lag statistic to one value per subject, then run either an **equivalence test (TOST)** against a pre-stated smallest effect size of interest (SESOI) at the subject level, or report a **Bayes factor** in favour of the null relative to the pre-stated SESOI. Report the subject-level sensitivity statement (minimum detectable effect at 80% power, n = 15) alongside the TOST result.
- Frame as a real, reportable finding at the subject level: "no subject-level EDA-HR coupling-lag effect larger than [SESOI] is detectable at this sample size"; do *not* frame as "well-powered null". The specific hypothesized signature isn't there at the magnitude the window-level test could detect, but the paper has no basis to rule out smaller subject-level effects without the TOST/Bayes analysis above.

### 5.2 Calibration under missing modalities (H-B1) — primary comparison

**Section renamed from "Robustness under missing modalities" per peer-review C1:** the word "robustness" implied a property the data do not demonstrate at n=15 subjects (overlapping CIs, Nadeau–Bengio-corrected p≈0.15). The section still reports the same measurements; the name now describes what is measured (calibration under missing modalities), not what is claimed about the model.

**Primary endpoint change (per peer-review C5, MA1, MA2).** This section's primary endpoint has been migrated from "ECE_reduced / ECE_complete ratio" to a four-metric reduced-condition panel: (1) Brier score, (2) debiased equal-mass ECE (Roelofs et al., AISTATS 2022; Bröcker 2012; Ferro & Fricker 2012), (3) AUROC, (4) AUPRC. Rationale, in short:
- The ratio inflated whenever ECE_complete was small; because the gated model is marginally *worse*-calibrated on complete data (0.098 vs LightGBM's 0.087), it was mechanically rewarded by the ratio (MA1).
- A per-tier ECG-only model has an ECE ratio ≈1 by construction (it never sees EDA), which makes any ratio-based comparison with the gated model structurally unfair to the per-tier model (MA2).
- Calibration without discrimination is uninformative — a model that falls back to the base rate when EDA is removed has low ECE and is useless (C5; see also PULSE's 0.994 AUROC without EDA as the reference bar).

Absolute reduced-condition Brier plus debiased ECE are therefore primary, AUROC/AUPRC are co-primary, and the ratio and ΔECE are secondary summaries kept for continuity with the prior-version numbers.

**Baseline set actually run (per peer-review C4; see `run_baselines.py` and `results/baselines.json`):**
- (i) **LightGBM-NaN-at-test** — complete-training LightGBM, NaN (not zero) delivered at test time so native missing-value handling kicks in. Tests whether the headline LightGBM baseline was doubly handicapped by zero-fill.
- (ii) **LightGBM-NaN-training p=0.5** — LightGBM trained with random EDA→NaN masking at the same rate as the dropout training. The apples-to-apples tree counterpart of the gated+dropout condition.
- (iii) **Per-configuration ECG-only LightGBM** — a LightGBM trained on ECG features only and served when EDA is missing. The obvious deployment baseline and a hard bound on what a single "robust" model can achieve.
- (iv) **Mean-imputation + per-condition recalibration** — mean-imputation for missing EDA with separate Platt calibrators for the complete and reduced conditions. Isolates imputation effects from architectural effects.
- (hr) **HR-only** (new per peer-review MA3) — single-feature classifier using `hrv_mean_nn` alone. Bounds how much the multimodal architecture adds beyond heart rate under missingness. If the HR-only baseline matches the gated model's reduced-condition Brier/AUROC, the architectural contribution collapses to "heart rate is a stable feature in WESAD."
- (hr-removed) **H-B1 without `hrv_mean_nn`** (per MA3) — gated model with `hrv_mean_nn` removed from the ECG branch, to check whether calibration behaviour survives when the single HR feature is unavailable.
- (hr-resid) **H-B1 with HR-residualized HRV** (per MA3) — regress each HRV feature on `hrv_mean_nn` within subject and feed the residuals to the gated model; tests whether the apparent fallback hierarchy is anything other than heart rate expressed in different units.
- (v) **PULSE-style distillation** — deferred to the venue version (requires an EDA-teacher distillation setup; labelled future work in Section 11.2 per peer-review C4).
- (vi) **CML regularizer (Ma et al., ICML 2023)** — deferred to the venue version for the same reason.

**Pre-stated falsification rule (per peer-review Strength #1).** Specified before baselines (i)–(iv) and (hr) executed; the baseline-set plan and this rule are registered on OSF (DOI to add) and tagged in git (`baselines-plan-v1`) with the dated artifact listed in the decision-timeline appendix. The rule:

> *If the gated architecture (with and without dropout) does not beat baselines (ii) EDA-NaN-training LightGBM and (iii) per-configuration ECG-only LightGBM on reduced-condition absolute Brier **and** debiased ECE **and** AUROC, the architectural claim does not hold and the paper is reframed as a candid evaluation paper. Specifically, in that case we recast the contribution as 'under this evaluation protocol, deploying a reduced-sensor model is as good or better than a single gated model, and the apparent gated-model advantage reflects reliance on heart rate (MA3) rather than architecture.'*

The rule is reported whichever way it fires; both outcomes are publishable per 10.1.

Per-fold ECE ratios:
- Dropout: [1.54, 1.56, 3.32, 1.02, 0.60]
- No-dropout: [2.01, 1.69, 4.43, 2.19, 0.74]

- Cohen's d_z (paired): mean_delta / SD_delta = −0.604 / 0.509 = **−1.19** (equivalently t/√n = −2.662/√5 = −1.19). By Cohen's convention |d| > 0.8 is a large effect, so the standardized effect size is unambiguously large despite the sample-size-limited p-value.

**Wilcoxon-floor argument (reviewer-requested).** At n=5 non-tied pairs with all differences pointing the same way, the two-sided exact Wilcoxon signed-rank p is 2 · (1/2⁵) = 2/32 = **0.0625** — this is the *minimum attainable* two-sided p at this sample size. Our observed p = 0.063 (0.0625 rounded) is not "p happened to land above α" but "p is at the floor by construction; no evidence more extreme than what we observed can push it below α = 0.05 without adding subjects." Combined with W = 0 (perfect rank consistency) and d_z = −1.19 (large standardized effect), this is a stronger and more precise statement than the previous "underpowered but consistent" framing.

**Design-level consequence:** specifying a test (two-sided Wilcoxon at n=5) that cannot reject at α = 0.05 is a design flaw, not a result. The venue version uses **20× repeated 5-fold subject-level CV** (Bouckaert & Frank — recommended for replicability) plus a subject-level sign-flip permutation on per-subject proper-scoring-rule differences (n=15, 2^15 = 32,768 sign patterns, floor ≈ 6×10⁻⁵), both of which lift the achievable minimum p well below α (per peer-review MA5). Report the single-fold Wilcoxon result as it stands but do not treat the α-ceiling as evidence either way.

**Nadeau-Bengio corrected paired t (reviewer-flagged — compute and quote this before submission).** With 3 test subjects and 12 training subjects per fold, the Nadeau-Bengio correction factor is (1/5 + 3/12) = 0.45 instead of the naive 1/5 = 0.2. The corrected SE grows by √(0.45/0.2) = 1.5×, so t = −2.662 becomes approximately −1.77 (df = 4), giving p ≈ 0.15 — not 0.056. The current "underpowered, p = 0.056" framing therefore overstates the inferential status. The accurate one-liner is: **"after correcting for CV dependence (Nadeau-Bengio), the paired dropout-vs-no-dropout contrast is p ≈ 0.15 (d_z = −1.19, direction consistent across all 5 folds, Wilcoxon at the exact floor); the point estimate is a direction for future work, not a statistically separated effect at this sample size."**

Honest framing: the direction is consistent, the Wilcoxon result is at the sample-size-imposed floor, the effect size is large, and yet p > 0.05 at n=5 by construction and p ≈ 0.15 under the Nadeau-Bengio correction. ### 5.4 Representation learning (H-B2) — null result + structural finding
- Contrastive pretraining: no measurable benefit at current scale (~15 subjects)
- Structural gap finding (see Section 2) reported here as the dataset-level contribution. **Per peer-review m7, the "no public dataset pairs confirmed panic attacks and confirmed cardiac events" claim is backed by a dated search appendix** (search terms, databases, cutoff date, included and excluded candidates). Without that appendix the claim is unverifiable; with it, the structural gap is a citable, reproducible contribution. If the paper is reposted after the search cutoff, the appendix must be updated or the claim restated as "no such dataset at the time of the previous search, dated [DATE]".
- **Causal attribution — downgrade from "the audit explains H-B2" to "consistent with":** H-B2's pretraining is *intra-WESAD cross-modal* (ECG↔EDA within one dataset; see 4.2). MIT-BIH has no EDA and therefore contributes no pairs to the contrastive loss. The audit's cross-dataset (WESAD-vs-MIT-BIH) shortcut separability (Section 5.6, Branch C) is therefore not mechanistically responsible for an *intra-WESAD* pretraining null — those are different experimental surfaces. The null is **consistent with** two non-exclusive explanations, and this paper does not have evidence to rank them:
  - (i) ~15 WESAD subjects is far too few for an InfoNCE objective to benefit downstream classification (the sample-size alternative — simplest and most likely);
  - (ii) if cross-dataset ECG-only pretraining were ever attempted on top of the intra-WESAD cross-modal arm, the audit's Branch C shortcut-separability (also partial/inconclusive, so weak ground on its own) would plausibly interfere with alignment.
  State in the paper: "H-B2's intra-WESAD cross-modal null is consistent with the small WESAD subject pool (~15); the audit's partial cross-dataset separability (Branch C) is a plausible interference only to the extent that any future cross-dataset pretraining arm is attempted — the current H-B2 arm is intra-WESAD only." The earlier framing — "the audit explains why H-B2 was foreseeably null" / "you cannot align two datasets whose class labels are the datasets" — was a post-hoc causal story that conflated two experimental setups and is retracted.
  - Discriminating experiment (not run; future work): pretrain on a large external ECG/PPG corpus (e.g. PTB-XL, MIMIC-waveforms) and test whether the H-B2 null persists on the WESAD downstream task. If the null persists at larger pretraining N, the sample-size story is less tenable.

**MMD specification (locked per peer-review m8).** As implemented in `audit/step5_mmd.py` and reported in the venue version:
- **Kernel:** Gaussian RBF, k(x,y) = exp(−||x−y||² / (2σ²)) (confirmed in `audit/step5_mmd.py::_rbf_kernel`).
- **Bandwidth selection:** median heuristic on the pooled sample (σ = 2.55 in the 2026-09-30 run). *The median heuristic is computed on the same data as the test* — this is standard practice but inflates Type I error, so the paper reports both the single-σ test and the MMDAgg multi-bandwidth aggregation (0.5×, 1×, 2×, 4×, 8× the median, Holm-corrected across bandwidths; `audit/step5_mmd.py::mmdagg_holm`). The venue version additionally reports a held-out-subject σ check (median heuristic computed on a 3-subject hold-out, test on the rest) as a sensitivity for the data-dependent-bandwidth concern.
- **Block structure:** **subject blocks**, carrying all of each subject's windows together when permuting dataset labels (`audit/step5_mmd.py::block_permutation_p`). This preserves within-subject dependence; window-level permutation would be anti-conservative.
- **R > 2 "trivially separable" cut-off rationale:** `R = MMD²(C1) / p95(MMD² under N1/N2 subject-split nulls)`, i.e. the observed cross-dataset MMD scaled by the 95th percentile of the within-dataset-null MMD; R > 2 means the cross-dataset gap is more than twice the within-cohort subject-heterogeneity ceiling. ## 6. Discussion
- **What the data show (rewritten per peer-review C1 and Section 5 contradictions #5, #10, #11):** point estimates in which the gated model's ECE_reduced/ECE_complete ratio is smaller than a LightGBM baseline's at n=15 WESAD subjects, with 95% CIs that each include 1 (no degradation) and overlap one another; the dropout contribution is directionally consistent across all 5 folds but is not statistically separated under the Nadeau–Bengio correction (p≈0.15). Whether the gated architecture also beats stronger baselines (per-configuration ECG-only LightGBM, HR-only, NaN-training LightGBM) on *absolute* reduced-condition Brier, debiased ECE and AUROC is the venue-version's main question; the pre-stated falsification rule in 5.2 commits the paper to reporting that outcome either way. Equity, calibration-robustness, and pre-registration-discipline claims are *not* supported by these data and are not asserted.
- What's explicitly not established: panic-vs-cardiac differentiation in real patients; equity across real device tiers; calibration-robustness as a general property.
- Why the heart-rate dominance is a clinical concern (per peer-review Section 8): tachycardia is shared by panic, SVT, AF with rapid ventricular response and ischaemia, so a heart-rate fallback is least informative exactly at the decision boundary that motivates the company (per Section 8.3). This is reported alongside the H-B1 result, not only in Limitations.
- Calibration matters for safety only through abstention (per peer-review Section 8.4): aggregate calibration does not guarantee useful selective prediction (Lechuga López et al., 2026). The venue version adds risk–coverage curves under the reduced condition.

## 7. Limitations (reinforces, doesn't introduce for the first time, the proxy-data ceiling)
- No dataset pairs the target conditions — restated plainly, not softened
- Simulated device tiers, not real hardware populations
- Small N for contrastive pretraining (~15 subjects)
- SQI/artifact gating not yet validated against real ambulatory (non-research-grade) signal
- **Ablation modality substitution — pipeline deviation, not a dataset limitation (corrected per reviewer):** The pre-registered per-modality ablation named four conditions — ECG-only, EDA-only, PPG-only, respiration-only. Our current `feature_extraction.py` emits ECG and EDA features only, so PPG-only and respiration-only were substituted with ECG-only and no-dropout. **WESAD does contain the raw channels** (RespiBAN respiration at 700 Hz; E4 BVP/PPG at 64 Hz) — the substitution reflects engineering scope, not an inherent dataset constraint. Earlier outline wording calling the PPG/respiration ablations "not executable on this dataset" was factually wrong and is retracted. Resolving this before submission means either extracting E4 BVP and RespiBAN respiration from the raw distribution and running the pre-registered conditions, or disclosing the substitution as a HARKing-risk-adjacent protocol deviation. The PPG omission also undercuts the H-C1 device-tier story: the most realistic "lower tier" wearable is a wrist-only device (E4 = BVP + EDA + TEMP), which is exactly what the current pipeline cannot represent. Preferred tier redefinition (per reviewer): Tier 1 = chest ECG + EDA; Tier 2 = wrist PPG + EDA; Tier 3 = wrist PPG only. H-C1 as currently reported uses simulated tiers derived from the ECG+EDA feature set, which does not instantiate any of Tiers 2 or 3 as defined above.
- **Wide variance in ECE ratios across folds — state the reason, honestly (corrected per reviewer):** With 15 subjects and 5-fold subject-level GroupKFold, each test fold contains approximately 3 subjects. ECE computed on ~3 subjects' worth of windows is inherently unstable: a single subject whose physiology makes the classification problem harder or easier in a given tier can swing the fold's ECE ratio from <1× to >3×. The counterintuitive per-fold values (fold 5: ratio 0.60×, fold 3: ratio 3.32×) reflect **both** between-subject heterogeneity **and** ECE estimation noise at ~90 windows per fold (ratio instability when ECE_complete sits near 0.03-0.04, see 5.2 metric note). Earlier wording "this reflects real uncertainty, not measurement error" was misleading; much of the fold-to-fold variance *is* estimator noise. Honest statement: "the 95% CI on the ECE ratio spans [0.32, 2.90] because each held-out fold contains approximately 3 subjects **and** because ECE at ~90 windows per fold is a noisy estimator (especially when ECE_complete lands near 0.03-0.04). The naive fold-based interval probably still *understates* the true uncertainty because folds share ~75% of training subjects (Varoquaux 2018)."
- **Dropout effect size underpowered (per peer-review Section 5 contradiction #6):** The paired comparison (dropout vs. no-dropout) is uncorrected paired t p=0.056; Nadeau–Bengio-corrected p ≈ 0.15; exact Wilcoxon floor 0.0625 at n=5 (so the Wilcoxon p=0.063 is at the design-imposed floor, not a measurement). Directionally consistent across all 5 folds (Wilcoxon W=0) but not statistically separated after correcting for CV dependence. This is a real limitation on the dropout-specific claim; adequately powered replication would require substantially more subjects. Earlier Limitations wording cited only p=0.056; corrected here to cite both (uncorrected and NB-corrected) so the Discussion/Limitations/5.2b readings match.
- **H2a SESOI (per peer-review m5):** H2a's TOST equivalence test needs an a priori smallest-effect-size-of-interest. At n=15 subjects, paired, α=0.05, 80% power, the minimum detectable paired-subject effect is d_z ≈ 0.78 (verified in peer-review 6.1). Any SESOI below 0.78 is unidentifiable at this sample size, so the paper pre-states SESOI = 0.80 (slightly above the minimum detectable effect, Cohen "large") and labels the H2a result as "equivalence to within d_z = 0.80 could not be rejected", not as a demonstrated null. The SESOI and its rationale are in the decision-timeline appendix.
- **Age-based positivity violation (audit-relevant):** WESAD subjects are young (mean 27.5 ± 2.4 years); MIT-BIH subjects span 23-89 years, with a very different age distribution. Age drives HRV independently of clinical state. This is an irreducible covariate that no preprocessing can remove — the harmonization ladder (Section 5.6, `audit/harmonize.py`) addresses sampling rate, filtering, mains, amplitude, and spectral tape artifacts, but not demographics. Any cross-dataset conclusion is conditional on how the audit (5.6) handles this; the intra-dataset conclusions (H-B1, H-C1) are unaffected.
- **Audit thresholds are our operational proposal, not literature standard (updated per peer-review C2 and Section 5 contradiction #8):** The AUC bands, the R ≤ 1 heterogeneity criterion, and the Step 3 cut-offs in `configs/preregistration.yaml` were specified in a configuration file prior to the reported runs; the file is not independently timestamped, and the N2 null p95 exceeding the SUBSTANTIALLY cut-off (0.71 > 0.70) suggests the thresholds may have been informed by earlier null data. The venue version adopts null-relative thresholds via a dated post-hoc amendment, and the configuration file is being committed with a git tag plus an OSF registration before the next audit run so that the amendment and the pre-amendment thresholds both have dated artefacts. There is no published consensus threshold for this setting.
- **Residual cross-dataset signal after harmonization (from the 2026-09-30 audit run, Section 5.6):** even with F_ECG restriction, resampling to a common rate, and dropping the spectral triple (LF/HF/LF-HF ratio), the C1 subject-level identity AUC only moves 0.82 → 0.78, and MMD² remains significant at R = 1.67 above the within-dataset null p95. Concretely: the top identity-driving feature is `hrv_mean_nn` (mean NN interval), which is also WESAD's dominant clinical signal for stress. This dual role prevents a clean "drop the feature" fix without collapsing C3. The full leave-one-stage-out attribution (which the `--fast` run skipped) is scheduled as an overnight rerun and will identify whether H3/H4/H5/H7 close the residual gap; report those numbers before submission. Until then, name `hrv_mean_nn` explicitly as a feature whose cross-dataset comparability is confounded by demographics.

## 8. Conclusion
- **Restate as a methods contribution (rewritten per peer-review C1, Section 5 contradiction #10, and the Section 5.2 falsification-rule outcome):** on 15 WESAD subjects under subject-level 5-fold cross-validation, a modality-gated architecture has a smaller point-estimate ECE_reduced/ECE_complete ratio than a complete-trained *zero-fill* LightGBM baseline (4.23× → 2.21× → 1.61× for the baseline, no-dropout gated, and gated + dropout respectively), but on absolute reduced-condition Brier, debiased ECE and AUROC the gated model does not beat (ii) a LightGBM trained with NaN-masking at the same rate, (iii) a per-configuration ECG-only LightGBM, or (iv) mean-imputation + per-condition recalibration. The pre-stated falsification rule in Section 5.2 fires. The reframed contribution is therefore (a) a candid negative result against stronger baselines, (b) a HR-only sensitivity analysis showing that nearly all of the gated model's reduced-condition discrimination comes from `hrv_mean_nn` alone (removing it drops reduced AUROC from 0.881 to 0.706), and (c) a decision-timeline + protocol that other wearable groups can reuse. The dropout-vs-no-dropout contrast is directionally consistent across folds but not statistically separated under the Nadeau–Bengio correction (p ≈ 0.15). Under *simulated* device tiers (H-C1), the gated model's absolute accuracy loss (7.5%) is smaller than the baseline's (10.6%); we characterise this as a smaller accuracy loss under simulated tiers, not as equity. All reported H-B1/H-C1 effects are within-WESAD; the audit (5.6) landed at Branch C (partial/inconclusive) so cross-dataset claims are hedged by design. The paper no longer uses the term "pre-registered" in the Conclusion (per peer-review C2); methodological choices are described in the decision-timeline appendix with their dated artefacts where available.
- Real cardiac-vs-panic differentiation requires a clinically-labelled cohort — named directly as the next step, not implied.
